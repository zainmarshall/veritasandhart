After a major acquisition, the tempting question for human resources is: how do we integrate? My client's situation called for a better one: what is the business trying to win, and can the talent system deliver it?

### The situation

A Fortune 100 technology company had made a major acquisition in service of an ambitious market position, and the talent implications were immediate. The engineering skills the strategy depended on most were 15 to 40 percent under-compensated against specialized competitors. Offer decline rates in the scarcest skill were running 80 to 90 percent. A vesting cliff for acquired talent was months away. Underneath the urgent symptoms sat a structural cause: the job architecture collapsed the critical skills into broad families like general engineering, which made accurate benchmarking impossible no matter how capable the compensation team was.

### What I did

Veritas & Hart was engaged for a roughly three month diagnostic and capability build spanning compensation, benefits, culture, talent acquisition, and retention. I framed one question for human resources leadership: can the company attract, retain, and develop the talent its strategy requires?

I designed a three input triangulation: interviews with engineering leaders, validation sessions with the recruiters who live the market daily, and an external signal drawn from partner discussions, advisory firms, and published research. From that, I identified and rated 22 critical engineering skills across four technology pillars, at the skill level rather than the job family level, a first for the organization. I redesigned the compensation peer set into three distinct groups, separating product competitors from companies that compete only for the same talent, and secured approval to submit it for benchmarking.

When the compensation work surfaced the job architecture problem underneath it, I resisted the pull to narrowly answer the compensation question. Instead I built a simple two by two decision framework, internal distinctness against external distinctness, to determine where a pay premium within an existing family suffices and where a new job family is warranted. I also surfaced two themes that hiring alone cannot solve, internal mobility and workforce planning that accounts for artificial intelligence, and scoped a single team pilot to prove the workforce planning methodology before anyone bet the enterprise on it.

Deliberately, everything was delivered in a research findings posture rather than as declarative recommendations. The stakeholders held different mandates and different analytical bars, and findings people can interrogate build more durable alignment than answers they are asked to accept.

### What happened

The redesigned peer set was approved and submitted for benchmarking refresh on schedule. The job architecture point of view was ready for board review on its timeline. The 22 critical skills are now visible in skill level conversations for the first time, and the time-sensitive compensation response shipped before the vesting cliff. Just as important to me: the methodology, the scoring model, the decision framework, and the pilot playbook were built as reusable assets the client's team can operate without me.
